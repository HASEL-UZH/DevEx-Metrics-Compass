How was the Compass created? • Who created the compass?
The Compass curates metrics from scientific literature and public company resources (engineering blogs, case studies, and talks from organizations such as Google, Microsoft, Atlassian, and Spotify), bringing together perspectives from both research and industry. Each metric is coded across dimensions including data collection method, source context, company size, framework or company of origin, AI impact assessment, and outcome goal.
The Compass was created by Dr. André N. Meyer, Patrick Meyer, Prof. Dr. Gail C. Murphy, and Prof. Dr. Thomas Fritz as part of research at the University of Zurich (UZH) and the University of British Columbia (UBC).

Why we created the Compass

With more than 130 developer experience metrics in circulation across research and industry, the challenge for most teams is not a lack of options. It is knowing which metrics are right for their specific context, maturity, and goals. The Compass helps teams at any stage of their measurement journey:

  • New to DevEx measurement. The guided wizard narrows 130+ metrics down to a manageable first set based on your context, available data, and outcome goals.
  • Running an existing program. Map your current metrics to identify dimensional coverage gaps, then benchmark against frameworks like SPACE and DX Core 4 or against what organizations like Google, Microsoft, and Stripe report measuring.
  • Researchers and dedicated DevEx teams. The full open-source dataset lets you filter by source (research vs. industry), compare frameworks, and identify understudied dimensions and metrics that may be worth pursuing.
Who created the Compass

The Compass was created by Dr. André N. Meyer, Patrick Meyer, Prof. Dr. Gail C. Murphy, and Prof. Dr. Thomas Fritz as part of research at the HASEL Lab, University of Zurich (UZH) and the University of British Columbia (UBC).

How we created the Compass

The Compass is built on a systematic analysis of 130+ metrics drawn from scientific literature and the public practices of more than 50 engineering organizations (including Google, Microsoft, Atlassian, Spotify, Lloyds, and Stripe), covering engineering blogs, conference talks, case studies, and podcasts. Each metric is annotated across multiple dimensions: how data is collected (automated or self-reported), collection maturity (getting started through advanced), the framework or company of origin, AI relevance, and outcome goal (Developer Experience, Product Excellence, or Organizational Effectiveness).
